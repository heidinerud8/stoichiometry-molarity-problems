Stoichiometry Problem Set: Finding Original Molarity (Standalone HTML version)

Overview

In these problems, a solution reacts to form a solid precipitate. The precipitate is collected by filtration, dried, and weighed. Using the mass of the solid product and stoichiometry, you will work backward to determine the original molarity (M) of a reactant in solution.

General Strategy

  1. Write a balanced chemical equation for the reaction.
  2. Convert the mass of the solid product to moles using its molar mass.
  3. Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation to find moles of the reactant of interest.
  4. Calculate molarity using: M = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution

Problems

Problem 1

A 25.00 mL sample of silver nitrate (AgNO3) solution of unknown concentration reacts completely with excess sodium chloride (NaCl). The solid silver chloride (AgCl) precipitate is collected, dried, and found to have a mass of 0.717 g. What was the molarity of the original AgNO3 solution?

Problem 2

A 50.00 mL sample of a barium chloride (BaCl2) solution is treated with excess sulfuric acid (H2SO4). The barium sulfate (BaSO4) precipitate that forms is filtered, dried, and weighs 1.167 g. Calculate the molarity of the BaCl2 solution.

Problem 3

A 100.0 mL sample of calcium nitrate (Ca(NO3)2) solution reacts with excess sodium carbonate (Na2CO3). The calcium carbonate (CaCO3) precipitate is collected and has a mass of 0.500 g. What was the original molarity of the calcium nitrate solution?

Problem 4

A 20.00 mL sample of lead(II) nitrate (Pb(NO3)2) solution is mixed with excess potassium iodide (KI). The lead(II) iodide (PbI2) precipitate weighs 0.461 g after drying. Determine the molarity of the Pb(NO3)2 solution.

Problem 5

A student has a sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution of unknown concentration. They take a 40.00 mL sample and add excess magnesium chloride (MgCl2). The magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)2) precipitate that forms weighs 0.291 g. What is the molarity of the NaOH solution?

Hint: Pay attention to the mole ratio!

Problem 6

A 75.00 mL sample of potassium bromide (KBr) solution is reacted with excess silver nitrate (AgNO3). The silver bromide (AgBr) precipitate has a mass of 1.878 g. Calculate the molarity of the original KBr solution.

Problem 7

A 30.00 mL sample of copper(II) sulfate (CuSO4) solution reacts with excess hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas. The copper(II) sulfide (CuS) precipitate weighs 0.478 g. Find the molarity of the CuSO4 solution.

Problem 8

A 60.00 mL sample of aluminum chloride (AlCl3) solution is treated with excess sodium hydroxide (NaOH) under controlled conditions. The aluminum hydroxide (Al(OH)3) precipitate is collected and weighs 0.780 g. What was the molarity of the AlCl3 solution?

Problem 9 (Challenge)

A 50.00 mL sample of an iron(III) chloride (FeCl3) solution is reacted with excess sodium hydroxide. The iron(III) hydroxide precipitate is collected and then heated strongly to convert it entirely to iron(III) oxide (Fe2O3), which weighs 0.400 g. Calculate the molarity of the original FeCl3 solution.

Hint: You need two balanced equations — one for the precipitation and one for the decomposition.

Problem 10 (Challenge)

A 45.00 mL sample of zinc nitrate (Zn(NO3)2) solution reacts with excess sodium phosphate (Na3PO4). The zinc phosphate (Zn3(PO4)2) precipitate weighs 0.579 g. Determine the molarity of the Zn(NO3)2 solution.

Hint: The mole ratio is not 1:1!
