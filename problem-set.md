# Stoichiometry Problem Set: Finding Original Molarity

## Overview
In these problems, a solution reacts to form a **solid precipitate**. The precipitate is collected by filtration, dried, and weighed. Using the **mass of the solid product** and **stoichiometry**, you will work backward to determine the **original molarity (M)** of a reactant in solution.

## General Strategy
1. **Write a balanced chemical equation** for the reaction.
2. **Convert the mass of the solid product to moles** using its molar mass.
3. **Use the mole ratio** from the balanced equation to find moles of the reactant of interest.
4. **Calculate molarity** using: M = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution

## Problems

### Problem 1
A 25.00 mL sample of silver nitrate (AgNO₃) solution of unknown concentration reacts completely with excess sodium chloride (NaCl). The solid silver chloride (AgCl) precipitate is collected, dried, and found to have a mass of 0.717 g. What was the molarity of the original AgNO₃ solution?

---

### Problem 2
A 50.00 mL sample of a barium chloride (BaCl₂) solution is treated with excess sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄). The barium sulfate (BaSO₄) precipitate that forms is filtered, dried, and weighs 1.167 g. Calculate the molarity of the BaCl₂ solution.

---

### Problem 3
A 100.0 mL sample of calcium nitrate (Ca(NO₃)₂) solution reacts with excess sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃). The calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) precipitate is collected and has a mass of 0.500 g. What was the original molarity of the calcium nitrate solution?

---

### Problem 4
A 20.00 mL sample of lead(II) nitrate (Pb(NO₃)₂) solution is mixed with excess potassium iodide (KI). The lead(II) iodide (PbI₂) precipitate weighs 0.461 g after drying. Determine the molarity of the Pb(NO₃)₂ solution.

---

### Problem 5
A student has a sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution of unknown concentration. They take a 40.00 mL sample and add excess magnesium chloride (MgCl₂). The magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)₂) precipitate that forms weighs 0.291 g. What is the molarity of the NaOH solution?

*Hint: Pay attention to the mole ratio!*

---

### Problem 6
A 75.00 mL sample of potassium bromide (KBr) solution is reacted with excess silver nitrate (AgNO₃). The silver bromide (AgBr) precipitate has a mass of 1.878 g. Calculate the molarity of the original KBr solution.

---

### Problem 7
A 30.00 mL sample of copper(II) sulfate (CuSO₄) solution reacts with excess hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) gas. The copper(II) sulfide (CuS) precipitate weighs 0.478 g. Find the molarity of the CuSO₄ solution.

---

### Problem 8
A 60.00 mL sample of aluminum chloride (AlCl₃) solution is treated with excess sodium hydroxide (NaOH) under controlled conditions. The aluminum hydroxide (Al(OH)₃) precipitate is collected and weighs 0.780 g. What was the molarity of the AlCl₃ solution?

---

### Problem 9 (Challenge)
A 50.00 mL sample of an iron(III) chloride (FeCl₃) solution is reacted with excess sodium hydroxide. The iron(III) hydroxide precipitate is collected and then **heated strongly** to convert it entirely to iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃), which weighs 0.400 g. Calculate the molarity of the original FeCl₃ solution.

*Hint: You need two balanced equations — one for the precipitation and one for the decomposition.*

---

### Problem 10 (Challenge)
A 45.00 mL sample of zinc nitrate (Zn(NO₃)₂) solution reacts with excess sodium phosphate (Na₃PO₄). The zinc phosphate (Zn₃(PO₄)₂) precipitate weighs 0.579 g. Determine the molarity of the Zn(NO₃)₂ solution.

*Hint: The mole ratio is not 1:1!*
